PROBLEM STATEMENT
02 / 10
THE TASK
Reading emotion from a 48×48 grayscale face.
A multi-class image classification problem with seven possible labels — and a lot of ways to get it wrong.
A face showing fear can look almost identical to one showing surprise — and at 48 pixels, the difference can disappear entirely.
WHY IT'S HARD
Expressions vary across people, lighting, and angle. Subtle features — a furrowed brow, a half-smile — are washed out by low resolution.
WHY IT MATTERS
Applications in healthcare, accessibility, customer experience, and HCI — wherever a system needs to read affect.
OUR QUESTION
Which deep learning techniques helped the most, and why?

--- Slide 4 ---
METHODOLOGY
04 / 10
PREPROCESSING & TRAINING SETUP
A clean, controlled pipeline — one variable at a time.
i
Image Preparation
Read as grayscale via OpenCV, resized to 48×48, reshaped to (48,48,1), normalized to [0,1].
ii
Label Encoding
Emotion indices converted to one-hot vectors using Keras categorical utilities.
iii
Class Weighting
Computed via sklearn's compute_class_weight("balanced") to fight the Disgust imbalance.
disgust → 8.96 · happy → 0.54
iv
Optimizer & Loss
Adam at lr=0.001 (default), categorical cross-entropy loss. Batch size 64.
v
Training Length
Max 30 epochs with EarlyStopping (patience 6) and ReduceLROnPlateau (factor 0.5, patience 3).
vi
Checkpointing
Best weights saved per run — letting us recover from Colab session crashes without restarting from scratch.

BEST MODEL
07 / 10
CNN + DATA AUGMENTATION
Our highest accuracy. Fewest parameters.

WHAT WE CHANGED
Kept the same regularized CNN, baked augmentation directly into the model with Keras layers — RandomFlip, RandomRotation (≤15°), RandomZoom (≤15%).
WHY IT WORKED
The model sees the same face at different angles, scales, and orientations during training — and learns that the emotion label doesn't change. More variety, less memorization.

WHAT IT GETS RIGHT
Happy & Surprise — visually distinctive features (a smile, an open mouth + raised brows) survive even at 48 pixels.
WHAT IT GETS WRONG
Fear overlaps with Surprise (wide eyes, open mouth) and Angry (furrowed brow) — subtle differences are lost at this resolution.
Disgust has high recall (0.71) but low precision (0.27) — the class weight pulled it forward, sometimes too aggressively.
ABLATION HEADLINE
Removing augmentation drops accuracy from 58.30% → 43.80% — a −14.5 pt swing. The single most important component in our best model.

--- Slide 9 ---
REAL-WORLD CONSIDERATIONS
09 / 10
DEPLOYMENT · BIAS · DATA
58% on a benchmark is not 58% in the wild.
DEPLOYMENT
Built to run, not yet to ship.
Real-time inference is feasible — 456K parameters fits on a phone — but accuracy drops under bad lighting, occlusion, and off-angle faces.
Quantization (int8) needed for smooth performance on low-end devices.
Real cameras output far higher resolution than 48×48 — a crop-and-align preprocessing step is essential.
BIAS & ETHICS
Not every face is in the training set.
Demographic bias — FER-2013 is known to perform unequally across ethnicity and gender.
Cultural difference — a "neutral" face in one culture may read as "sad" in another.
High-stakes use in hiring, surveillance, or law enforcement should not happen without human oversight.
DATA LIMITS
48 × 48 hides the signal.
Micro-expressions — the most informative cues — are simply invisible at this resolution.
Class imbalance — Disgust having 10× fewer samples is a data problem, not a model problem.
Crowd-sourced labels — expressions tend to be exaggerated and staged, not natural.
